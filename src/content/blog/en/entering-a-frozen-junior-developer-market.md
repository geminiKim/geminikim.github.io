The junior developer market in 2024 is difficult. Openings have narrowed, and experienced developers are also finding moves harder. Advice that ignores that condition can sound encouraging while giving a candidate nothing useful to do.

My reading is that the preceding hiring boom changed company behavior. Many firms hired rapidly when developers were scarce, then faced weaker investment conditions, reorganizations, or enough existing staff that they no longer needed the same intake. When a company does hire, it can afford to raise the bar. This is an interpretation from experience, not a complete economic analysis, but the immediate effect on applicants is real.

## Broaden the first step

A candidate cannot control when the market improves. Waiting only for a preferred large company may leave no work experience to discuss when hiring returns. If openings are scarce, it may be rational to include smaller or less prominent companies in the search and begin accumulating professional experience sooner.

That is not an instruction to accept any job or to reproduce the low pay and conditions of someone else's career. Company quality, compensation, and personal circumstances still matter. It is a tradeoff the applicant must make. The narrower point is that a first role can create access to later roles because companies continue to value evidence from real work.

Choose a place where there are actual problems to solve. Learn what that company needs, finish work, and record the decisions you made. Experience is more useful than a calendar year when you can explain what changed because you were there.

## Write the reasons, not only the stack

A résumé that says “Spring, JPA” tells an interviewer very little. A stronger entry explains:

- what problem existed;
- why you chose a particular response;
- what was difficult;
- how you handled it;
- what you learned or would change.

This does not prove problem-solving ability with certainty. No résumé or interview can do that. It gives the interviewer evidence to question, and it distinguishes work you understand from a list of tools copied from the project.

The same standard applies to personal projects. A project becomes more convincing when it has a purpose, a problem, a deployment, and decisions the candidate can defend. Elaborate architecture without a user or constraint supplies less evidence than a modest service whose tradeoffs are understood.

## Show active reasoning

The colleague I want to work with does not accept every statement because a senior person or famous developer made it. They ask why, form an opinion, and can disagree with reasons. This is not a demand for constant argument or unpaid overtime. It is a preference for someone who participates in the judgment rather than waiting to be directed at every step.

That is also why interviews benefit from questions without a single memorized answer. Knowledge questions matter, but answers can be rehearsed or generated quickly. A conditional design question lets the interviewer observe how a candidate clarifies assumptions and reaches a conclusion. Even then, interviews are imperfect; people are understood more accurately through working together.

## Build what the market can inspect

There is no fixed curriculum for each year of a backend career. In a constrained market, a more useful loop is to find work, solve the problems available there, study what those problems require, and translate the result into evidence for the next opportunity.

The market may open again, but an applicant cannot schedule that. The practical response in 2024 is to widen the set of acceptable starting points without abandoning judgment, then make each experience legible: the problem, the reason, the action, and the result. That does not remove the unfairness of a frozen junior market. It does create a path that depends on more than waiting.
