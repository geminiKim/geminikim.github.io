Consider an experienced developer whose three jobs have all been at large companies. The path has gone well, but the next step is unclear. Before recommending another company size, ask a more decisive question: what does this person want to be in five years?

## The answer splits at the future role

Someone who wants to become a deeply technical senior engineer or craft specialist may be well served by remaining in a large organization.

The recommendation changes for a future CTO, founder, or broad organizational leader. A person whose experience has been limited to well-known large companies can benefit from trying a much smaller company or startup. This is not advice for every developer; it targets experienced people whose current path has hidden a set of constraints their intended role will require.

## A small problem reveals a large-company habit

Large-company experience can make someone strong at large systems and large problems. It can also encourage large structures for small work, although this tendency does not apply to everyone. A smaller organization exposes whether the engineer can make a small solution with fewer resources and carry a wider share of the surrounding work.

This is experience with hardship: imperfect teams, difficult collaboration, and people who do not all work with the same skill or enthusiasm. In the speaker's view, future CTOs and founders need some meaningful experience of that hardship to understand the reality they may have to lead. Engineers who already gained that range in a difficult large-company team may not need the same move.

## Do not pay for the experiment with a salary cut

The condition is explicit: do not reduce compensation to enter the startup. Maintain it or improve it. Variety is not a reason to accept a lower salary in this recommendation. Career diversity also does not mean handling successively higher traffic in the same setting. It means changing the kind of problem, team, process, and responsibility you understand. If the desired future is broad leadership, missing constraints may be worth seeking at a smaller scale under the compensation floor. If the desired future is technical depth, staying on the large-company path is not a lesser choice; it is the path aligned with the goal.
