Two companies made offers at the same time, and one offered considerably more money. The other one, a webtoon service operating in Korea, Japan, and the United States, offered missing experience. It was a chance to learn how content, localization, country-specific rules, latency, caching, images, and traffic across time zones worked in a real service.

## The lower offer contained the missing experience

That choice was not based on the domain name alone. It was driven by concrete questions the role might let him answer. A similar pattern guided later moves: he sought B2C experience because he had not worked in B2C, and after working in other domains he wanted to see a financial system from the inside.

A large company was also chosen as an experience to test. Specialized teams and mature processes were useful to see, but the narrow scope hid infrastructure and incident work that had been part of the appeal. That outcome was still valuable because it replaced an imagined benefit with a preference learned firsthand.

## A company supplies material; the developer must use it

Experience is “material,” or more bluntly, “prey.” A company does not place growth directly into an employee. The developer has to investigate the work, take responsibility, and turn what happened into capability. A famous company or attractive domain is not sufficient if the role does not provide the relevant work, and exposure has little value if the person does not make it their own.

The conclusion is stronger than merely balancing salary and experience. In the long view, an engineer who is willing and able to work hard should normally put the next experience at the center of the decision, not immediate pay.

Immediate personal circumstances may require the current gain to decide the move. Otherwise, experience is the more useful long-term criterion, although a difficult hiring market can constrain the move and market conditions may later change.

The choice remains personal. Some engineers want narrow specialization; others want broader operating responsibility. Ask what this role can teach that the current path has not, and whether you are prepared to turn that opportunity into your own judgment. Money is visible at the offer stage. The experience becomes valuable only after you use it.
