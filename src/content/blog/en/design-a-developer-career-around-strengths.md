Several junior colleagues asked similar career questions during farewell coffee chats. The answer was not a job title or a ladder. It began with a slower task: learn what kind of developer you are, what work you prefer, and what future you currently want. Forming those answers took roughly six years of repeated thought.

## Find one sharp strength and two supporting ones

His working model is one clearly strong capability with two less sharp but useful strengths. Coding, driving work forward, and understanding a complex domain are examples of different contributions. A team works by combining such profiles; trying to be equally strong at everything hides the combination you actually offer.

List more than the obvious skill and rank your abilities honestly. If you cannot yet see them, ask colleagues what they rely on you for. Knowing weaknesses matters too: it lets you say which work you can lead and where another person's support would improve the result. Early-career developers may not have enough evidence yet, so this picture takes time.

## A preference becomes visible only after contact

“Backend” includes product services, B2B solutions, platforms, shared service teams, lower-level systems, and open source. Trying open-source contribution while considering that path made the difference concrete. Studying protocols and contributing was interesting, but the experience revealed a stronger preference for solving business and domain problems closer to users.

That conclusion could not have come from imagining the work. Company opportunities are best, but a side project or contribution can provide a partial experience when the real opportunity is unavailable. It will not reproduce full-time responsibility; it can still tell you whether a direction deserves a larger commitment.

## Revisit the future from today's position

A five-year picture is not a forecast fixed to one date. Ask again from today: where do I want to work, what problems do I want to solve, and what kind of developer—or other professional—do I want to become? Even a salary target can be a starting goal when nothing clearer comes to mind.

Spend even ten minutes reflecting on that future instead of never considering it. As experience changes your strengths and preferences, revise the picture and let it guide the next experience you seek.
